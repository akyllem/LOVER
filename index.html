The house

A small house with a long memory of the water.

Talasea began as a fisherman's cottage in 1962 and has grown — slowly, deliberately, never more than one wing at a time — into the twenty-four-room house you'll find today. Every room faces the sea. Every meal is shaped by what the boats brought in that morning.

We don't have a concierge desk, a gym, a lobby bar, or a brand standard. We have a dining room, a kitchen garden, three saltwater pools, and a staff of eleven who have, between them, worked here for two hundred and thirty-one years.

The rooms

Four ways to fall asleep listening to the sea.

Twenty-four rooms across the main house, the garden cottages, and the cliff suites. Every room has an open window, a real key, and a view of the bay.

The kitchen

Two seatings, no menu, whatever the boats brought.

Lunch is served from one. Dinner is served from eight. There is one menu and it is decided by Stavros at the harbour each morning. We will gladly accommodate, but we will not gladly be ordered.

The place

Two hours from anywhere, five minutes from the sea.

We sit on the western face of the island — far enough from the harbour town to feel quiet, close enough that you can walk in for an evening glass of wine and walk back along the path with the lanterns lit.

  • 3km

    The harbour town

    A walking village of 800 — bakery, two tavernas, the daily market, the small bookshop and post office.

  • 12km

    The chapel of Áyios Pétros

    A whitewashed chapel on the north headland, walkable through the olive grove. Sunrise is unforgivable.

  • 42km

    The mainland ferry

    Two crossings a day to the mainland. We can arrange the car, the boat, and the lunch en route.
