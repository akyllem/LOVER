In April 1962, Andreas Vlachos painted the front door of his late father-in-law's cottage a particular shade of pale green and hung a hand-lettered sign that read, simply, Talasea. There were six rooms. The first guest was a Dutch ornithologist who stayed eleven nights, paid in cash, and returned every spring for the next thirty-one years.

The hotel grew by accident, then on purpose. A second wing in 1971 â€” built around the surviving fig tree, which still bears fruit each August. A third in 1989. What we believe

That a hotel is a small, daily act of hospitality, repeated. That a room is improved by removing things from it. That dinner is the most important hour of any day, and should not be hurried. That guests should arrive tired and leave rested, and that anything else is a failure of the house.

We do not have a brand standard, a uniform, a key card, or a check-in time. We have a guest list, a phone, two seatings, and the same staff who were here last year and the year before that.

"Talasea is small, slow, and entirely the point. They did not flinch when we extended. Our taxi did."

How we work

The dining room serves two seatings, lunch and dinner, with a single menu decided each morning. Breakfast is laid out on the long table in the courtyard from seven, and you take what you'd like. There is one bar, in the dining room, open from before lunch until late. We are happy to bring a drink to your terrace if you'd prefer to read.

Reservations are confirmed by hand within forty-eight hours, by the same person, every time, in case there are questions about the rooms or the islands or the boats. We are open from the first of April to the eighth of November, and closed the rest of the year for the winter, the staff, and the slow work of repair.

What we believe

Three quiet rules of the house.

01

Subtract before you add.

Most rooms get worse the more you put in them. Each year we try to remove one object that the room does not need. So far: the room phone, the kettle, the printed menu, the directional signage, the doormat.

02

The hour around dinner is sacred.

We do not take check-ins between 7 and 9pm. We do not take phone calls. The kitchen is open and the dining room is full and that is what we are doing for the next two hours.

03

Keep the staff longer than the guests.

Eleven of us. Two hundred and thirty-one years between us. We are slow to hire, slow to change a recipe, slow to repaint, fast to apologise.
